Frequently Asked Questions

Why should fuel injectors never exceed 80% to 85% duty cycle?
At duty cycles over 85% to 90%, the electromagnetic solenoid in the injector does not have enough time to physically close between engine cycles. The injector begins to 'float' or stay locked wide open, causing fuel puddling, loss of fuel metering control, and sudden lean air/fuel mixture spikes that melt pistons.
Why does E85 ethanol require 30% larger fuel injectors than pump gas?
Ethanol has lower chemical energy density per gallon than pure gasoline. To achieve the correct stoichiometric combustion ratio, engines running on E85 must inject approximately **30% to 35% more liquid fuel volume** to produce the exact same horsepower, necessitating much larger injectors and high-flow fuel pumps.
What is Brake Specific Fuel Consumption (BSFC)?
BSFC measures how many pounds of fuel an engine consumes each hour to generate one horsepower ($lbs / hp \cdot hr$). Standard naturally aspirated engines have a BSFC of around 0.45 to 0.50. Forced induction turbo/supercharged engines require extra fuel for in-cylinder charge cooling and have a richer BSFC of 0.55 to 0.65.
